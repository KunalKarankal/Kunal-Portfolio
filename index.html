01 · Challenge

Repeat model creation consumes valuable engineering time.

When similar products are created repeatedly, engineers can spend too much time rebuilding familiar geometry, locating data, and applying the same decisions manually. A configurator must separate stable product rules from the values that change for each requirement.

The goal is to turn approved inputs into a useful model while keeping the design logic clear enough to maintain and extend.

This public case study explains the engineering approach only. Product data, internal business rules, and company-specific implementation details remain confidential.

02 · Contribution

Support parameter-driven model generation.

  • Assist in developing custom configurator automation tools using VB.NET.
  • Contributed to a housing configurator that generates ready-to-use models from simple input parameters.
  • Connect structured product inputs with repeatable CAD-model creation.
  • Clean and organize engineering data so automation can work from consistent, accessible information.
  • Review recurring workflow steps and identify where manual modeling and preparation effort can be reduced.
  • Coordinate with R&D and sales stakeholders around product requirements and faster working cycles.
  • Apply Value Analysis and Value Engineering thinking to identify cost-effective product and process improvements.

03 · Engineering logic

Build the workflow around controlled decisions.

The configurator approach can be understood as a sequence: collect the required inputs, map them to known design rules, update the model, and deliver a state that an engineer can continue using. The value comes from making the same approved logic repeatable rather than relying on memory or manual recreation.

INPUT

Structured parameters

Define which product values change and how they enter the workflow.

RULES

Engineering intent

Represent repeatable product decisions in clear automation logic.

MODEL

Parametric generation

Use the selected values to create or update useful CAD geometry.

HANDOFF

Ready-to-use result

Reduce setup work so engineers can focus on review and downstream needs.

04 · Value

Shorter design cycles through smart automation.

The configurator approach reduces manual modeling, improves data consistency, keeps productivity high, and gives engineers more time for product decisions, validation, and customer-specific exceptions.

Research case study

Design, fabrication & CFD analysis of fins.

A mechanical-engineering research project combining geometry development, Ansys Fluent analysis, fabrication, experimentation, and published findings.

← Back to all work
ProjectOct 2022 – May 2023
Result15% improvement
ToolsInventor · Ansys Workbench

01 · Research question

How can fin geometry improve heat transfer?

The project compared multiple fin configurations to understand how geometry influences thermal performance. The aim was not only to identify a stronger shape computationally, but also to confirm that the result remained meaningful when the design was fabricated and tested.

This created a complete engineering path from geometry definition to numerical comparison, physical validation, and publication.

02 · Method

Model, simulate, fabricate, validate.

  • Reviewed more than ten academic papers on heat sinks and fin optimization to guide geometry selection and parameter tuning.
  • Modeled multiple fin geometries in Autodesk Inventor for a controlled comparison.
  • Used Ansys Workbench for steady-state thermal and CFD-backed evaluation of temperature distribution and convection performance.
  • Compared configurations and optimized fin dimensions and arrangement for improved heat dissipation.
  • Fabricated physical prototypes of the strongest designs and performed controlled laboratory testing.
  • Benchmarked simulation results against experimental outcomes and confirmed close agreement.
  • Compiled, proofread, formatted, and aligned the findings with Springer publication requirements.

03 · Research workflow

Use multiple forms of evidence.

RESEARCH

10+ papers reviewed

Use prior thermal-system studies to guide geometry, variables, and analysis choices.

SIMULATION

Thermal comparison

Evaluate temperature distribution and convection performance before fabrication.

EXPERIMENT

Physical validation

Test prototypes and benchmark real measurements against the simulated result.

PUBLICATION

Technical writing

Convert the validated method and findings into a clear peer-reviewed book chapter.

04 · Result

15% higher heat-transfer coefficient.

The optimized fin geometry produced a measurable performance improvement, and experimental results closely supported the simulation. The work demonstrated an integrated CAD–CAE–prototype methodology and became a Springer book chapter in the AMPT 2023 proceedings, published in 2024.
